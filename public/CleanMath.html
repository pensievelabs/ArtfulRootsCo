The Manipulatives Kit

Six physical tools, reimagined for glass.

Each module is an open-ended Montessori material. No locked levels, no arbitrary gates. Children touch, drag, balance, and build true number sense.

01

The Number Grid — Subitizing & Pattern Vision

Subitizing is the foundational ability to recognize quantities instantly without counting one-by-one. The tactile grid helps children group dots into recognizable visual shapes—building instant mental math reflexes.

02

The Tens Frame — Visual Base-Ten Architecture

The core building block of all elementary arithmetic. Heavy, snap-to-slot counters give children a physical feel for how numbers combine to make 5 and 10. Abstract place-value becomes visually obvious.

03

The Bead Chain — Linear Sequence & Magnitude

A classic Montessori material brought to life. Children drag and connect strings of colored beads to physically experience the relative magnitude and order of numbers along a continuum.

04

The Fraction Pie — Parts of a Living Whole

Fractions shouldn't be introduced as confusing fraction bars on a worksheet. Children assemble interactive slices—halves, thirds, quarters, and eighths—seeing how parts construct a harmonious whole.

05

The Equivalence Scale — Mechanical Logic of Math

What does the equals sign (=) really mean? A responsive mechanical balance scale shows that mathematical equations are dynamic balances of quantity—making addition and subtraction physically tangible.

06

Number Bonds — Part-Whole Decomposition

Mastering how numbers break apart into components (e.g., 7 is 3 and 4, or 5 and 2). Builds the flexible mental decomposition required for advanced mental arithmetic without memorizing times-tables by rote.

Why This Tool Exists

I wanted my child to understand math, not fear the buzzer.

When I looked for early math apps for my young child, every top-rated option was built like a stressful speed arcade. Red countdown clocks, loud buzzer sound effects for mistakes, and endless confetti animations designed to provoke adrenaline rather than deep thought.

In Montessori classrooms, children don't use timers. They handle wooden beads, place tokens in wooden tens frames, and build spatial intuition with their hands. I built Clean Math as a software engineer and parent to bring that exact tangible calmness to digital screens.
